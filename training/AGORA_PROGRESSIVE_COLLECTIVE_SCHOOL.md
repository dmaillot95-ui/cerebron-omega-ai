## Goal
Make all colonies learn together progressively: understand fundamentals, ask each other useful questions, attempt answers, teach peers, receive corrective work, and advance only when measured comprehension is sufficient.

## Mentor loop
CÉRÉBRON:
1. selects a simple concept appropriate to each colony;
2. gives a short lesson and one worked example;
3. assigns individual comprehension questions;
4. assigns cross-colony questions;
5. collects answers with provenance;
6. grades against an explicit rubric;
7. diagnoses errors and misconceptions;
8. recalibrates difficulty;
9. assigns corrective exercises where needed;
10. advances difficulty only after mastery gates;
11. periodically gives blind transfer tests;
12. records progress without converting consensus into evidence.

## Difficulty ladder
L0 vocabulary / definitions
L1 direct comprehension
L2 simple application
L3 multi-step application
L4 cross-domain transfer
L5 contradiction / counterexample
L6 open-ended construction
L7 research-grade problem
L8 frontier/open problem

No colony starts at L7/L8 merely because its label is specialized.

## Peer learning
Each learning round may create:
STUDENT -> ANSWER
PEER -> QUESTION
PEER -> ALTERNATIVE_ANSWER
AUDITOR -> CHECK
COUNTER_AUDITOR -> ATTACK
REPRODUCER -> REBUILD
MENTOR -> CORRECTION
COLONY -> RETEST

A peer answer is educational input, not automatically truth.

## Question policy
Colonies are encouraged to ask:
- what they do not understand;
- why another colony used a method;
- what assumptions a result needs;
- whether a method transfers to their domain;
- which counterexample breaks a claim;
- what evidence would change a conclusion.

## Mastery gates
Suggested scoring dimensions, each 0-4:
UNDERSTANDING
CORRECTNESS
EVIDENCE
TRANSFER
ERROR_DETECTION
EXPLANATION
CALIBRATION

Promotion in difficulty requires:
- no critical factual/proof error;
- provenance preserved;
- satisfactory novel-task transfer;
- acceptable calibration;
- mentor/audit pass.

Scores guide training; they are not scientific evidence.

## Recalibration
If a colony fails:
DIAGNOSE -> SIMPLIFY -> TEACH -> EXAMPLE -> RETRY -> VARIANT -> RETEST.
If it succeeds repeatedly:
HARDER_VARIANT -> CROSS_COLONY_TASK -> BLIND_TRANSFER.
If it memorizes:
NEW_SURFACE_FORM -> NEW_CONTEXT -> SAME_UNDERLYING_SKILL.
If it becomes overconfident:
CALIBRATION_TASK -> EVIDENCE_LABELING -> UNKNOWN/OPEN exercises.

## Initial simple curriculum
SIGMA: definitions, implication/equivalence, quantifiers, small proofs.
ARGUS: identify one seeded logical or arithmetic error.
ANTITHESIS: construct a simple counterexample to a false universal claim.
REPLICA: independently reproduce a short derivation.
BENCHMARK: distinguish training example from unseen evaluation.
MEMORIA: retrieve a fact while preserving exact provenance.
CHEMIA: units, moles, balancing equations, basic thermochemistry.
MATERIA: distinguish strength/stiffness/toughness; simple material selection.
MOTORIS: torque, power, efficiency, simple energy balance.
INDUSTRIA: cycle time, throughput, bottleneck, simple reliability.
Other colonies receive analogous L0-L2 curricula when real resources are assigned.

## Cross-colony starter exercises
CHEMIA asks MATERIA how composition can change material properties.
MATERIA asks MOTORIS which properties limit a motor component.
MOTORIS asks THERMIA where losses become heat.
THERMIA asks INDUSTRIA how cooling affects production constraints.
INDUSTRIA asks METROLOGIA what must be measured to control quality.
SIGMA asks ARGUS to audit a short proof.
ARGUS asks ANTITHESIS to attack its accepted assumptions.
REPLICA independently rebuilds the surviving result.
MEMORIA stores only the audited lesson and its failure cases.

## Mentor intervention rule
CÉRÉBRON may assign extra work whenever observed evidence shows:
knowledge gap;
repeated error;
poor calibration;
weak transfer;
shared-dependency echo;
contradiction;
benchmark leakage;
or an opportunity for useful cross-domain transfer.

## End state
The target is not identical colonies.
The target is complementary specialists that can teach, question, audit and help each other while retaining distinct expertise and independent evidence lineages.
